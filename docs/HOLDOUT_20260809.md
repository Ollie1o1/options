## 1. bull_put — the four new features

IC with clustered t in brackets. In-sample is 2022-24 (n=3,808), holdout is
2020-21 (n=1,913).

| feature | in-sample | holdout | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vol_of_vol` | +0.2418 (15.02) | +0.2255 (9.12) | sign and size both hold |
| `skew_25d` | −0.0905 (−4.18) | **−0.1707 (−4.84)** | sign holds and strengthens |
| `iv_velocity` | +0.0691 (2.86) | +0.0242 (0.75) | same sign, loses significance |
| `term_slope` | +0.0431 (2.42) | **−0.0568 (−1.49)** | **FLIPS** |

### `term_slope` failed, and it was the one with the best theory

Backwardation as a stress signal is the best-documented vol-timing result in
the public literature, it was the top recommendation in
`LEADING_INDICATORS_20260809.md` §3.1, and it inverts across the split. It is
also weak in both windows. Theory lost to data; that is the whole point of
running the holdout before wiring anything.

The stated caveat is the likely reason and it does not rescue the feature: this
cache is DTE 10-67, so the measurable slope is roughly 10d against 60d rather
than the 1M/3M the literature means. **That is a data wall, not a fixable
specification** — nothing on disk can test the real quantity. Revisit only if
optionsDX lands, which carries all expirations.

### `vol_of_vol` survives but is probably arithmetic

Sign and magnitude both hold, at clustered t of 15.0 and 9.1. That looks like
the strongest result here until it is put beside its neighbours in the same
table:

| feature | in-sample IC | holdout IC |
|---|---|---|
| `atm_iv` | +0.3243 | +0.3346 |
| `rv` | +0.2890 | +0.2459 |
| `vol_of_vol` | +0.2418 | +0.2255 |

Three measures of "how volatile is this name", all stable, all the same sign
and rough size. For a credit spread held to expiry that expires worthless
80-89% of the time, higher IV at a fixed delta means more credit for the same
width, and RoC is then a deterministic increasing function of the credit. That
is **the identical mechanism §3 of `ATTRIBUTION_20260808.md` used to throw out
`credit_pct_width`**, and `credit_pct_width` sits at the top of both tables
(+0.5711 / +0.7541) exactly as it should if this is the same fact three more
times.

The shape test agrees it is not a graded effect: `vol_of_vol` reads `mono`
+0.10 in-sample and +0.50 on the holdout — no consistent ordering across the
parameter, which is what an identity looks like and not what a signal looks
like. **Do not treat `vol_of_vol` as a discovery.**

### `skew_25d` is the one worth keeping

The IC holds its sign and gets stronger out of sample (−0.0905 → −0.1707,
clustered t −4.18 → −4.84, p < 0.0001 in both), and unlike the three above it
is **not** a volatility-level proxy — it is a cross-strike shape, and its sign
is opposite to `atm_iv`'s in the same table, so it cannot be the same fact.

The direction is the informative part. A **richer put wing predicts a WORSE
bull put**. That is not the naive read (more premium for the wing you sell) but
it is the same pattern §5 already found: you were paid more precisely because
the risk was real. Steep skew is the market pricing genuine downside, and on a
bull put you are the one selling it.

**It is still not promotable, and the reason is specific.** The IC holds but
the *shape* does not: `q_spread` goes −0.0335 → +0.0083 and `mono` goes −0.70 →
+0.20. A feature that rank-orders consistently while its quantile ordering
inverts is not yet understood, and this repo's own history says the quantile
screen is the one that catches what the IC misses, not the other way round.

## 2. The call side, holdout-tested for the first time

| feature | in-sample | holdout | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `long_delta` | +0.0398 (3.11) | +0.0837 (4.34) | **sign holds** |
| `skew_25d` | +0.0148 (0.46) | +0.1663 (5.57) | flat then strong — inconsistent |
| `vol_of_vol` | −0.0565 (−4.58) | +0.1279 (4.36) | **FLIPS** |
| `term_slope` | −0.0419 (−2.48) | −0.0107 (−0.71) | same sign, fades |
| `iv_rank` | +0.0145 (0.44) | −0.0182 (−0.94) | flat in both |

**`long_delta` survives the test it was set.** The worry in
`LEADING_INDICATORS_20260809.md` §4 was that 2022-24 is a mega-cap rally, so
"higher delta did better" and "the market went up" are the same sentence. The
holdout contains a 34% crash *and* the largest melt-up in the sample, and the
sign holds with clustered t above 3 in both windows.

**But it is five times smaller than §4 of `ATTRIBUTION_20260808.md` reported.**
That document quotes IC **+0.2349** at n=180, on the capped MEGA cohort. At
proper n it is **+0.0398 / +0.0837**. The large figure was a small-sample
artifact of the concurrency cap, the same defect §4d-bis found in the IV-rank
numbers. §4's long-call table should be read with that correction.

`vol_of_vol` flipping hard between structures, having "held" on the put side,
is further evidence it is measuring credit rather than risk.

## 4. Nothing predicts the disaster — now measured, not asserted

`tail_auc` ran on all 18 features across both windows and both structures.
**Not one reading is far from 0.5**; the extremes are `iv_rank` at 0.593 and
`trend` at 0.411 on the bull_put holdout, and neither would survive an 18-way
correction.

§5 of `ATTRIBUTION_20260808.md` reached this by hand on 11 features in one
window. It now holds on 18 features, two windows, two structures, and it is a
column that prints on every future run rather than a study someone has to
remember to redo.

## 5. Two numbers that will be misread if left unqualified

**`bull_put` returns +4.09% on the holdout (clustered t 3.84, DSR 1.000).** Do
NOT read that as "short premium survived COVID". The window is two years of
which the crash is about five weeks, and the recovery dominates the average.
`TAIL_OBSERVED_20260808.md` measured what happened to spreads actually open
*into* the crash: every one lost 100% of capital at risk. Both are true, and
the second is the one that sizes positions.

**`long_call` returns +16.50% on the holdout (DSR 0.999).** 2020-21 is close to
the best two-year window in history for owning calls. That is a regime read,
not an edge, and the in-sample window has the same strategy at +0.52% and
`[reject]`.

Nothing here is wired into scoring, and nothing here should be. The next
question for `skew_25d` is why a stable IC sits on an unstable quantile shape —
until that is answered it is an observation, not a signal.

And the standing bound is unchanged: every number above assumes an entry
crosses the full quoted spread, which has still never been measured.
